Boston College Student Research & Funding Guide

How to get paid, earn course credit, and fund your own research at Boston College. Every item links to or comes from an official BC page (checked November 2026).

Paid · University-wide

Undergraduate Research Fellowship (URF)

BC pays undergraduates to assist on a faculty member's research project. The professor applies for the funding, not you, so the first step is always finding a professor who wants your help.

  • Funding requests come from the faculty member whose project you'll assist
  • Reviewed and funded three times a year: fall, spring, and summer
  • Ordinarily paid work, not academic credit
  • Some departments add their own process (Economics has a central application; see its department note)
Official URF page
Academic Credit

Research for Course Credit

Each department runs its own research and thesis courses. You usually need a faculty mentor and a signed contract first. Examples from department pages:

  • PSYC2205 / PSYC2206: Psychology research or independent study (about 8-10 hrs/week, up to 3 credits)
  • Biology: research for credit, 3 credits/semester (10+ hrs/week); thesis BIOL4962
  • CSCI4961: CS Honors Thesis (two sections, senior year)
  • EESC5595: Earth & Environmental Sciences Senior Thesis
  • NURS4911: Nursing independent study (pass/fail)
Pick a department above to see its note. You generally can't be paid and get credit for the same hours.
More Funding

Grants for Your Own Research

BC's University Fellowships Committee also funds student-driven research:

  • Advanced Study Grants: student-designed summer projects, including thesis research (requires a faculty nominator)
  • Conference Travel Grants: up to 50% of costs ($500 max) to present your research
  • Several travel and research fellowships for specific regions and topics
See all programs and deadlines
Etiquette

Outreach Best Practices

BC professors receive dozens of generic inquiries. Here is how to stand out:

  • Email from your official @bc.edu account
  • Mention a specific recent paper of theirs (listed on each card)
  • Follow their preferred application method (Google Form vs. Email)
  • Attach your resume and unofficial degree audit
  • Check the lab's undergrad badges and when that evidence is from before sending
